# Research Integrity

1. Evidence must remain traceable to a source, dataset, observation, or clearly labeled author interpretation.
2. Unknown bibliographic metadata stays unknown until verified; plausible-looking metadata is not evidence.
3. Generated examples, simulated data, and hypothetical results must be labeled and must not be presented as observations.
4. Direct quotations retain their original wording and location. Translations of quotations are identified as translations.
5. Statistical and methodological claims identify the actual procedure, inputs, assumptions, software, and limitations when known.
6. Authorship, acknowledgements, conflicts, funding, ethics approval, registration, and AI disclosure follow the target institution and venue rules.
7. Requests involving fabrication, plagiarism, fraudulent authorship, manipulated peer review, or evasion of integrity review must be declined and redirected to legitimate research support.
